Model definition

Begin with the complete mathematical problem

Before interpreting a curve, identify the domain, unknowns, equations, boundary conditions, inputs, and outputs. These statements define what the simulation does—and what it cannot claim.

Problem statement. For a specified junction bias Vj and illumination scale s, solve the internal one-dimensional silicon PDE model for electrostatic potential and carrier densities. Then calculate contact current and apply the external terminal model. {ψ(x), n(x), p(x)} = S(θdevice; Vj, s) Jdevice = −[Jn(0)+Jp(0)]  →  {Vterm, J} = T(Jdevice; Rs, Rsh) Here S is the coupled DEVSIM drift–diffusion solve and T is the explicit per-unit-area terminal mapping. This separation prevents circuit parameters from being mistaken for material physics.

Solved self-consistently

DEVSIM solves ψ, n, and p from Poisson and carrier continuity. Scharfetter–Gummel edge currents couple the three fields.

Prescribed model inputs

Geometry, abrupt doping, mobilities, lifetimes, Auger coefficients, optical bins, reflectance, and effective dead-layer losses are supplied rather than inferred.

Calculated after the PDE solve

Rs/Rsh terminal mapping, J–V metrics, measurement residuals, and the model–data adequacy gate do not alter the semiconductor PDE equations.

0 · Domain, unknowns, and assumptions

What exactly does DEVSIM solve—and under which assumptions?

The device is one silicon region on the depth coordinate 0 ≤ x ≤ L. Light enters at x = 0 through the front p+ emitter; the n-type base extends to the rear contact at x = L.

DomainL = 250 µm; metallurgical junction xe = 0.5 µm.
Unknownsψ(x), n(x), and p(x): electrostatic potential, electron density, and hole density.
Fixed chargeNA = 1019 cm−3 in the p+ emitter and ND = 1017 cm−3 in the n-type base.

Abrupt doping input: NA(x) = NA0H(xe−x) and ND(x) = ND0[1−H(xe−x)], where H is the step function.

Geometry note: the depletion region is a solution of Poisson’s equation around xe, not a third material layer. “Depleted” means that mobile carriers are scarce while ionized dopants remain; it does not mean an intrinsic silicon layer. The device is an ordinary p–n junction, not a p–i–n structure.

VT ≈ 25.9 mVThermal voltage at the supported temperature T = 300 K.
Vbi ≈ 0.95 VFrom VTln(NAND/ni2) using the same Boltzmann approximation as the model.
W0 ≈ 0.109 µmZero-bias depletion estimate: about 0.108 µm in the n base and 1.1 nm in the p+ emitter.

Use these estimates as checks, not measurements: they explain why the junction is strongly one-sided. The nominal 1019 cm−3 emitter is also where the omitted degeneracy and band-gap-narrowing physics matter most.

  • Steady state and isothermal: no time derivatives; T = 300 K throughout.
  • One dimensional: lateral current spreading, fingers, busbars, and edge leakage are omitted.
  • Semiconductor statistics: Boltzmann statistics, complete dopant ionization, and DEVSIM’s 300 K silicon constants.
  • Transport: constant low-field mobilities with the Einstein relation; no field- or doping-dependent mobility.
  • Optics: prescribed 16-bin, front-only, single-pass Beer–Lambert absorption; no rear reflection or light trapping.
  • High-doping limitation: band-gap narrowing and Fermi–Dirac degeneracy in the nominal p+ emitter are not modeled.

Unit convention: the solver uses cm–V–s units: densities in cm−3, electric field in V/cm, G and R in cm−3s−1, and current density in A/cm2.

1 · Governing equations

Which equations are solved in the silicon?

There are three unknown fields and three coupled conservation equations. After the current laws, generation, and recombination relations are substituted, these equations form the nonlinear boundary-value problem solved at every operating point.

Core PDE system
d/dx(ϵSidψ/dx) = −q(p − n + ND+ − NA−) dJn/dx = q(R − G) dJp/dx = −q(R − G)

DEVSIM discretizes this continuum system with a one-dimensional finite-volume formulation and solves ψ, n, and p self-consistently. Carrier currents use the stable Scharfetter–Gummel Bernoulli edge form; the equations shown here are the continuum laws it represents. The following constitutive and source relations close the system.

Closure relationEquation used in this projectPhysical role
Charge and field ρ = q(p − n + ND+ − NA−)E = −dψ/dxND+ ≈ ND,   NA− ≈ NA Couples mobile and fixed charge to potential, band bending, depletion charge, and electric field.
Drift–diffusion currents Jn = qμnnE + qDndn/dxJp = qμppE − qDpdp/dxDn,p = μn,pVT Defines electron and hole transport by electric-field drift and density-gradient diffusion.
Optical generation G(x) = s(1 − Rf) ΣiΦiαie−αix Prescribes electron–hole-pair generation from 350–1100 nm using 16 single-pass Beer–Lambert bins.
Recombination and loss R = RSRH + RAuger + Rfronteff + RbackeffRSRH = (np − ni2)/[τp(n+n1) + τn(p+p1)]RAuger = (Cnn+Cpp)(np−ni2)Rfronteff = (Sf/δ)(n−ni2/pp+)H(δ−x)Rbackeff = (Sb/δ)(p−ni2/nn)H(x−L+δ) Bulk SRH/Auger loss plus effective volumetric loss inside thin front and rear dead layers.

Definitions: VT = kBT/q; n1 and p1 are the SRH trap reference densities (both equal ni in the baseline); pp+ and nn are the local equilibrium majority-carrier densities in the emitter and base; and δ = 0.2 µm is the effective loss-layer thickness.

Implementation boundary: Rfronteff and Rbackeff are volumetric source terms, not interface boundary conditions. The optical field is prescribed and is not solved self-consistently with the carrier equations.

2 · Boundary and operating conditions

What is imposed at x = 0 and x = L?

Both ends use the ideal ohmic-contact models supplied by DEVSIM simple_physics. Carrier densities are pinned to the local equilibrium values implied by doping; the electrostatic potential contains the equilibrium contact potential plus the applied contact bias.

Front p+ contact · x = 0

ncpc = ni2,   nc − pc = ND − NA

n(0) = nc,front,   p(0) = pc,front

ψc = Vfront − VTln(pc/ni)

Vfront = Vj. Illumination enters only from this side. Vj > 0 is forward bias and lowers the equilibrium junction barrier.

Rear n contact · x = L

ncpc = ni2,   nc − pc = ND − NA

n(L) = nc,rear,   p(L) = pc,rear

ψc = Vback + VTln(nc/ni)

Vback = 0 is the electrical reference. No rear optical incidence or reflection is modeled.

Boundary-condition count: ψ, n, and p are each constrained at both contacts. These six Dirichlet conditions close the three second-order equations obtained after substituting the drift–diffusion current relations.

Operating caseElectrical conditionOptical conditionPurpose
Thermal equilibriumVfront = Vback = 0s = 0Establish the built-in potential; Jn ≈ Jp ≈ 0.
Dark J–VVfront = Vj, Vback = 0s = 0Probe diode transport and recombination without photogeneration.
Illuminated J–VVfront = Vj, Vback = 0G = sG0(x)Obtain Jsc, Voc, maximum power, and fill factor.
EQEVj = 0; external Rs/Rsh mapping disabledActivate one wavelength bin at a timeCompute EQEi = Jsc,i/(qsΦi): collected electrons per incident photon in bin i.

Sign convention: V = Vfront(p) − Vback(n), and Jout = −(Jn+Jp) at the front contact. Therefore illuminated Jsc is positive and forward dark current is negative.

External terminal model: Rs and Rsh are not PDE boundary conditions. After the internal DEVSIM solve, J = Jdevice(Vj) − Vj/Rsh and Vterm = Vj − JRs. In the configuration, shunt_resistance = 0 is an explicit sentinel for Rsh = ∞, not a physical zero-ohm shunt.

Forward-model inputs

  • geometry and abrupt p+/n doping;
  • 300 K mobilities, lifetimes, Auger coefficients, and effective loss velocities;
  • 16-bin spectrum, effective scale photon_flux, and front reflectance;
  • mesh, illumination ramp, junction-voltage sweep, Rs, and Rsh.

Forward-model outputs

  • internal doping, ψ, E, n, p, G, Jn, and Jp profiles;
  • dark and illuminated terminal J–V curves;
  • Jsc, Voc, maximum power, fill factor, and a clearly labeled illustrative efficiency;
  • wavelength-resolved EQE and its integrated-Jsc consistency check.

Additional calibration inputs

  • processed illuminated and dark J–V data;
  • separately acquired illuminated Jsc and Voc observations;
  • residual scales, block weights, parameter bounds, and vary/fix choices.

Calibration outputs

  • fitted effective generation scale and Rs;
  • predicted observables, per-block residuals, and model–data adequacy result;
  • local covariance diagnostics and an explicit identifiability warning.

Data boundary: raw instrument files are not direct PDE inputs. They are standardized first into voltage, current density, polarity, sample identity, illuminated area, and measurement conditions; only then are compatible observables compared with the model.

Area is experimental metadata: the bundled Cell #3 data use A = 3 cm × 4 cm = 12 cm2, with J = I/A and Ptotal = AVJ. Area does not enter the one-dimensional per-unit-area PDE solve.

Efficiency boundary: the code’s 0.1 W/cm2 input-power value is an illustrative reporting convention, not a value derived from the compact 16-bin spectrum and not a measurement of the laboratory irradiance. Experimental efficiency requires an independently calibrated cell-plane irradiance.

4 · Numerical solution sequence

In what order is the coupled problem solved?

  1. Build the nonuniform mesh and abrupt doping profile.
  2. Solve equilibrium Poisson with both external contact biases at zero.
  3. Introduce n and p and converge the dark drift–diffusion system.
  4. Add generation and recombination, then ramp illumination from zero to the requested scale.
  5. Ramp the front-contact junction bias with hidden continuation steps no larger than 0.05 V.
  6. Read the contact currents, apply the generation-positive sign convention, and map the internal-device curve through Rs/Rsh.

Why continuation? each converged state initializes the next nearby nonlinear problem. This is a numerical strategy, not an additional physical equation.

Mechanism walkthrough

Now interpret the model one mechanism at a time

The complete problem above states what is solved. The five questions below explain why each term is present, what it changes physically, and which simulation result should confirm the prediction.

01 · StructureDoping and junctionp+ emitter on an n-type base
02 · ElectrostaticsPotential and fieldSpace charge bends the bands
03 · IlluminationCarrier generationAbsorption depends on depth and wavelength
04 · TransportDrift, diffusion, lossCarriers move or recombine
05 · ObservationJ–V and EQEContacts convert internal physics into data
The self-consistent loop solved by DEVSIM (n, p, ND, NA) → ρ → ψ → E → (Jn, Jp) → continuity → (n, p)

A one-dimensional finite-volume discretization and Newton iteration solve ψ, n, and p together. Light intensity and voltage are ramped gradually so each converged state initializes the next.

Question 1 · Junction electrostatics

Why does the junction contain an electric field?

d2ψ/dx2 = −(q/ϵSi)(p − n + ND+ − NA−) E = −dψ/dx

Symbols: ψ is electrostatic potential, ϵSi is silicon permittivity, and ND+/NA− are ionized dopant densities. The term in parentheses is the net charge density divided by q.

Carrier diffusion across the p+–n interface exposes fixed ionized dopants. Their space charge forms a depletion region on both sides of the metallurgical junction and, because the base is less heavily doped, mostly inside the n-type base.

Direction check: x runs from the front p+ emitter to the rear n contact, so the built-in field points from n to p (−x).

Question 2 · Optical generation

Where are electron–hole pairs generated?

G(x) = s(1 − Rf) Σi Φiαie−αix

The model distributes a 16-bin AM1.5-like teaching spectrum through silicon using single-pass Beer–Lambert absorption. Φi and αi are the photon flux and absorption coefficient of bin i; Rf is front reflectance; s is the effective photogeneration scale.

Depth check: strongly absorbed short wavelengths generate near the front; weakly absorbed near-band-gap photons penetrate deeper.

Question 3 · Drift–diffusion transport

How do generated carriers reach the contacts?

Jn = qμnnE + qDndn/dx Jp = qμppE − qDpdp/dx

Symbols: μn,p are mobilities and Dn,p are diffusion coefficients. The first term is drift driven by E; the second is diffusion driven by a carrier-density gradient.

In the depletion region, the built-in field sweeps holes toward the front p contact and electrons toward the rear n contact. In the quasi-neutral emitter and base, diffusion is essential: a carrier contributes only if it reaches a collecting region before recombining.

Plot connection: compare doping, electric field, carrier density, and generation in results/profiles.png.

Question 4 · Continuity and recombination

Why are some generated carriers lost?

dJn/dx = q(R − G) dJp/dx = −q(R − G)
R = RSRH + RAuger + Rfronteff + Rbackeff RSRH = (np − ni2)/[τp(n+n1) + τn(p+p1)]

Symbols: G is the optical generation rate and R is the total recombination rate. The continuity equations require every local carrier gain or loss to be balanced by a change in current.

Continuity enforces the steady-state balance of generation, transport, and recombination. Larger bulk or near-contact loss reduces collection; at open circuit it also reduces quasi-Fermi-level splitting and therefore Voc.

Model boundary: the front and rear losses are finite-thickness effective “dead layers,” not full interface-state or Robin-boundary models.

Question 5 · From device solution to measured data

How does the internal solution become a measured J–V curve?

J = Jdevice(Vj) − Vj/Rsh Vterm = Vj − JRs

DEVSIM provides the internal junction response. The terminal mapping then adds area-normalized Rs and Rsh in Ω·cm2. This site uses generation-positive output: illuminated Jsc is positive, while forward dark-diode current is negative.

Short circuitVterm = 0; Jsc tests photocarrier collection.
Open circuitJ = 0; Voc reflects generation–recombination balance.
Maximum powerMaximize VJ; Rs and Rsh strongly affect fill factor.
Spectral responseJsc,EQE = q∫Φinc(λ)EQE(λ)dλ.

The Cell #3 joint objective uses illuminated J–V, dark J–V, separately acquired Jsc, and separately acquired Voc. Only s (photon_flux) and Rs vary so the exercise remains interpretable and does not claim information the measurements cannot support. Dark J–V is both a constraint on Rs at larger forward current and a cross-observable check on the fixed transport/recombination assumptions. It participates in the objective; it is not held-out validation data.

Quality score: define the normalized error eb = (ysim,b − ymeas,b)/σb and optimizer residual rb = √(wb/Nb)eb. Then Q = ||r||2/Σbwb = [Σbwb mean(eb2)]/Σbwb. The threshold Q = 4 is an instructor-defined teaching gate, equivalent to an aggregate normalized RMS √Q = 2; it is not a reduced χ2 test or confidence level. Failure flags model–data disagreement and requires both measurement and model checks.

Residual-to-decision check: Observe the residual pattern, verify measurement adequacy with two checks, test one directional single-factor hypothesis, and decide whether the fit supports physics or only demonstrates the workflow. Practice the chain in the Notebook and Lab Guide.

Model scope

Simple enough to learn; explicit enough to critique

This is a transparent teaching model for undergraduate students. It is not an industrial process deck or a claim of fabrication-grade parameter extraction.

  • Ordinary p+-on-n junction, not a p–i–n device.
  • One-dimensional transport with stated boundary conditions.
  • Single-pass optical absorption; no rear reflector or light trapping.
  • Only parameters supported by the available observations are fitted.
