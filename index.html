Coupled device physics

How absorbed light becomes terminal current

The equations are not separate chapters. Charge changes the electric field, the field and carrier gradients set current, and continuity updates the charge. Newton iteration closes this nonlinear loop.

The self-consistent loop solved by DEVSIM (n, p, ND, NA) → ρ → ψ → E → (Jn, Jp) → continuity → (n, p)

A one-dimensional finite-volume discretization and Newton iteration solve ψ, n, and p together. Light intensity and voltage are ramped gradually so each converged state initializes the next.

1 · Junction electrostatics

Space charge creates band bending

d2ψ/dx2 = −(q/ϵSi)(p − n + ND+ − NA−) E = −dψ/dx

Carrier diffusion across the p+–n interface exposes fixed ionized dopants. Their space charge forms a depletion region on both sides of the metallurgical junction and, because the base is less heavily doped, mostly inside the n-type base.

Direction check: x runs from the front p+ emitter to the rear n contact, so the built-in field points from n to p (−x).

2 · Optical generation

Absorption sets where pairs are created

G(x) = s(1 − Rf) Σi Φiαie−αix

The model distributes a 16-bin AM1.5-like teaching spectrum through silicon using single-pass Beer–Lambert absorption. Φi and αi are the photon flux and absorption coefficient of bin i; Rf is front reflectance; s is the effective photogeneration scale.

Depth check: strongly absorbed short wavelengths generate near the front; weakly absorbed near-band-gap photons penetrate deeper.

3 · Drift–diffusion transport

Fields and gradients move carriers

Jn = qμnnE + qDndn/dx Jp = qμppE − qDpdp/dx

In the depletion region, the built-in field sweeps holes toward the front p contact and electrons toward the rear n contact. In the quasi-neutral emitter and base, diffusion is essential: a carrier contributes only if it reaches a collecting region before recombining.

Plot connection: compare doping, electric field, carrier density, and generation in results/profiles.png.

4 · Continuity and recombination

Local conservation sets collection loss

dJn/dx = q(R − G) dJp/dx = −q(R − G)
R = RSRH + RAuger + Rfronteff + Rbackeff RSRH = (np − ni2)/[τp(n+n1) + τn(p+p1)]

Continuity enforces the steady-state balance of generation, transport, and recombination. Larger bulk or near-contact loss reduces collection; at open circuit it also reduces quasi-Fermi-level splitting and therefore Voc.

Model boundary: the front and rear losses are finite-thickness effective “dead layers,” not full interface-state or Robin-boundary models.

5 · From device solution to measured data

Contacts and parasitics define the terminal J–V curve

J = Jdevice(Vj) − Vj/Rsh Vterm = Vj − JRs

DEVSIM provides the intrinsic junction response. The terminal mapping then adds area-normalized series and shunt resistance. This site uses generation-positive output: illuminated Jsc is positive, while forward dark-diode current is negative.

Short circuitVterm = 0; Jsc tests photocarrier collection.
Open circuitJ = 0; Voc reflects generation–recombination balance.
Maximum powerMaximize VJ; Rs and Rsh strongly affect fill factor.

The Cell #3 joint objective uses illuminated J–V, dark J–V, independent Jsc, and independent Voc. Only s (photon_flux) and Rs vary so the undergraduate exercise remains interpretable and does not claim information the measurements cannot support. A failed residual block is evidence of model discrepancy, not permission to fit every parameter.

Model scope

Simple enough to learn; explicit enough to critique

This is a transparent teaching model for undergraduate students. It is not an industrial process deck or a claim of fabrication-grade parameter extraction.

  • Ordinary p+-on-n junction, not a p–i–n device.
  • One-dimensional transport with stated boundary conditions.
  • Single-pass optical absorption; no rear reflector or light trapping.
  • Only parameters supported by the available observations are fitted.
