DEVSIM in semiconductor TCAD

What DEVSIM provides

mesh + model expressions + discrete equations + nonlinear solver
  • finite-volume simulation in 1D, 2D and 3D
  • Python control of DC, small-signal AC and transient analyses
  • user-defined equations and inspectable internal fields
  • Apache 2.0 source code and text-based model scripts

What the modeler must provide

  • geometry, materials and doping
  • transport, recombination and optical models
  • contact equations, applied sources and sign conventions
  • mesh, solver tolerances and validation tests

DEVSIM solves the equations that we define. A converged solve does not prove that those equations represent the experiment.

References: DEVSIM project · DEVSIM Manual v2.10.0

Device, coordinates and external circuit

Orientation and signs

  • light enters at x = 0 through the p+ emitter
  • the metallurgical junction lies at x = 0.5 µm
  • the n-type base occupies nearly all of the 250 µm absorber
  • photogenerated holes reach the front; electrons reach the rear
  • generation-positive J gives output power P = VtermJ in the first quadrant

The depletion region forms around the junction after electrostatic equilibrium. It is not a separate material layer.

A device simulation is a boundary-value problem. Inputs and contact conditions define the problem; DEVSIM solves the internal state; terminal post-processing produces quantities that can be compared with measurements.

“Depleted” describes mobile-carrier removal near a junction. It does not mean that this ordinary p–n cell contains intrinsic silicon.

Poisson and drift–diffusion equations

Electrostatics

d/dx(ε dψ/dx) = −q(p − n + ND − NA)

Charge bends the electrostatic potential. The field follows from E = −dψ/dx.

Carrier currents

Jn = qµn(nE + VT dn/dx) Jp = qµp(pE − VT dp/dx)

Drift responds to the field. Diffusion responds to concentration gradients.

Steady-state conservation

dJn/dx = q(R − G) dJp/dx = −q(R − G)

Generation, recombination and current divergence must balance locally.

Self-consistent coupling

Potential changes transport. Carrier densities change charge. Charge then changes potential. DEVSIM solves the coupled residual rather than treating each equation independently.

Prediction before simulation: where should the strongest electric field occur, and what sign should it have for x increasing from p+ to n?

Generation and recombination

Single-pass optical generation

Gλ(x) = Φλ(1 − Rf)αλ exp(−αλx)
  • large α at short wavelength gives shallow absorption
  • small α near the band edge gives deep penetration
  • the current model omits rear optical reflection and texture

Carrier losses

RSRH = (np − ni2)/[τp(n + ni) + τn(p + ni)] RAuger = (Cnn + Cpp)(np − ni2)

SRH recombination connects the result to effective carrier lifetime. Auger recombination becomes important at high carrier or doping concentrations.

The project represents front and rear near-contact loss with thin effective loss regions. front_srv and back_srv are teaching proxies, not Robin surface-recombination boundary conditions.

Boundary conditions and terminal mapping

Ideal ohmic contacts

Vback = 0,   V = Vfront − Vback n|contact = neq,   p|contact = peq
  • positive V forward-biases the p+-on-n junction
  • the contact models close ψ, n and p at both ends
  • equilibrium starts with both external biases at zero

External terminal model

J = Jdevice(Vj) − Vj/Rsh Vterm = Vj − JRs,   I = AJ
  • Rs and Rsh use Ω·cm² because the model works with current density
  • Rsh = 0 in config.py denotes an infinite shunt resistance
  • area converts J to measured current but does not alter the 1D PDE solution

Short circuit sets Vterm = 0. Open circuit sets I = 0. Maximum power occurs at the J–V point that maximizes VtermJ.

Numerical solution

Where the algebra comes from

Unknowns are stored at nodes. Edge fluxes connect neighboring nodes. Integrating each conservation law over Ωi converts the PDEs into nonlinear algebraic residuals.

Finite-volume balance

∫Ωᵢ ∇·J dΩ = Ji+½ − Ji−½

Each control volume conserves charge through its edge fluxes and local source terms.

Scharfetter–Gummel flux

Jn,i+½ = (qµnVT/Δx)[ni+1B(ξ) − niB(−ξ)]

ξ = Δψ/VT. The Bernoulli edge form treats drift and diffusion together across steep potential changes.

Newton iteration

JF(uk)Δu = −F(uk)

For u = [ψ, n, p], DEVSIM assembles the residual and Jacobian until the coupled system converges.

Convergence is one numerical check. Repeat the calculation with a finer mesh and confirm that Jsc, Voc and the internal profiles remain stable.

Calibration as an inverse problem

Forward calculation

θ and applied V   ⟼   ysim = S(θ)

Known model parameters produce predicted J–V curves, scalar observables and internal fields.

Inverse calculation

measured y   ⟼   plausible θ

The inverse direction can be non-unique. Several loss mechanisms may create similar changes in one illuminated J–V curve.

Sensitivity before fitting

python scripts/plot_sweep.py --param hole_lifetime --start 1e-6 --stop 1e-4 --n 5

Question before optimization

Does the measured observable change enough when the parameter changes?

  • hole lifetime mainly affects minority-carrier collection in the n-type base
  • Jsc responds until another loss mechanism dominates
  • a weak or correlated response cannot support a unique fitted value

An optimizer cannot recover information that the measurements do not contain.

Student workflow

Build the forward model first. Check its internal physics. Add experimental data only after units, signs, illuminated area and irradiance are documented.

1 · Reproducerun the baseline scripts and Notebook
2 · Explainconnect each result to an equation or boundary condition
3 · Replace datapreserve raw files and create validated processed tables
4 · Calibratetest sensitivity, fit few parameters and report mismatch

A useful result states what the model explains, what the data constrain and what remains uncertain.
